CAC — Calculated Accurate Clarity

Rebuilding trust in an AI prediction experts had stopped believing — by closing the loop between production data, user behaviour, and the model.

Clarity correctness16% → 87%
ScopeIndia & Israel
My roleInitiative lead

Context

Sarine's planning platform predicts the clarity grade of a polished stone directly from a scan of the rough — a prediction planners depend on to decide how to cut. Clarity is a major driver of a stone's value, so getting it right shapes every downstream decision.

When the prediction is wrong often enough, planners stop trusting the automation and fall back to slow manual judgment — which quietly undermines the product's entire value proposition.

The problem

Predicted clarity was correct only about 16% of the time. But the real difficulty wasn't a single broken component — it was that “correctness” itself had to be defined, measured against real production outcomes, and validated by the people who actually make the cutting calls. There was no clean ground-truth signal sitting in a database waiting to be used.

My role & process

I led the improvement initiative across teams in India and Israel, acting as the bridge between planners, the demo team, and engineering. My work broke into three loops:

  • Production data collection — structuring how real outcomes were captured so the model could be measured against reality, not assumptions.
  • Planner validation — sitting with planners to understand how they actually judge clarity and where the system diverged from expert reasoning.
  • Customer feedback analysis — turning patterns in selection behaviour into prioritized, concrete requirements engineering could act on.

The hardest part was translation: converting tacit manufacturing behaviour and clarity-selection patterns into specifications a distributed engineering team could build against.

Outcome

Clarity correctness rose from 16% to 87%, restoring planner trust in the automated output and bringing the product's core promise back to life.

What I learned

Model accuracy is a product problem, not only a data-science one. The validation loop and the stakeholder translation mattered as much as any algorithmic change — because a prediction only creates value once the expert using it believes it.
